PlotStack vs Substack Insights for Substack analytics

Checked 2026-10-31 on the vendors' sites and Substack's help center · Independent, no affiliate relationship with either tool

The short answer: Both are read-only Chrome extensions that work from your logged-in Substack session and keep data in your browser. Pick Substack Insights for a quick, simple answer to what works and when to publish (free under 1,000 subscribers, $9.99 one-time above). Pick PlotStack if you want everything in one detailed dashboard, including growth sources, retention and per-Note signups, at no cost, and don't mind a very new one-person tool that doesn't support custom domains yet.

Check Substack's own stats first

Substack's dashboard already shows your subscriber growth, each post's opens and signups, and per-Note stats (open a Note and click View stats). An extension is worth installing when you want those numbers side by side, over time, or exported, instead of clicking through one post or Note at a time.

Simple answers vs a full dashboard

Substack Insights answers a few questions well: which posts land, which convert, and when to publish. PlotStack gathers almost everything Substack spreads across its stats, growth, posts and Notes screens into one place and writes short findings, such as a Note far above your usual or your main source of new readers. If you'd rather not dig through charts, the simpler tool is the better one.

What both have in common

Neither asks for your password and neither posts anything. Both read your stats through the Substack session already open in Chrome, which is how most Substack analytics tools work because Substack has no public stats API. That also means either can break when Substack changes its site; your built-in dashboard is always the fallback.

Our pick

Under 1,000 subscribers, try both: they cost nothing. Above 1,000, PlotStack gives you Notes and conversion stats for free that Substack Insights charges $9.99 for, as long as your publication is on a substack.com address. Substack Insights is the steadier choice if you want a polished, well-rated tool with less to learn.

Our verdicts

  • PlotStack: A free, read-only Chrome dashboard that pulls together what Substack spreads across several screens (subscribers, open rate, acquisition sources, cohort retention, per-post and per-Note stats, attributed signups) and points out what stands out compared with your own past numbers. No account and no server: data stays in your browser, and paid and revenue figures are hidden by default. It reads Substack's undocumented internal endpoints through your logged-in session and syncs once a day in the background, it doesn't support custom domains yet, and it's a very new one-person project with no Chrome Web Store ratings yet, so expect breakage when Substack changes things.
  • Substack Insights: A simple, inexpensive Chrome side panel that ranks your posts and Notes and exports a CSV with ready-made AI prompts. Analysis runs in your browser using your logged-in Substack session; it only reads, never posts. Small and new (v1.0 on the Chrome Web Store).
