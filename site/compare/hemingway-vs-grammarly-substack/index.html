Hemingway Editor vs Grammarly for Substack posts

Checked 2026-11-01 on the vendors' sites and Substack's help center · Independent, no affiliate relationship with either tool

The short answer: They do different jobs, and both free versions are worth using. Grammarly catches spelling, grammar and tone mistakes as you type, including in the Substack editor through its browser extension. Hemingway Editor's free readability checker shows your reading grade level and highlights hard-to-read sentences, which helps with dense posts. If you only pay for one, Grammarly Pro (from $12/mo) suits writers who want sentence rewrites everywhere they write; Hemingway Editor Plus (from $8.33/mo billed yearly) suits writers whose main problem is long, tangled sentences.

Two different questions

Grammarly answers "is this correct?" Hemingway answers "is this easy to read?" A post can pass Grammarly with no errors and still lose readers in a 60-word opening sentence, and a post can read smoothly and still have typos. That's why plenty of writers use both free versions rather than choosing.

A simple pre-publish routine

Write in the Substack editor with Grammarly's free extension on to catch typos as you go. Before you publish, paste your intro into Hemingway's free readability checker and fix the red sentences, since the opening is where readers decide whether to keep going. That takes about five minutes and costs nothing.

Keep your voice

Readers subscribe for how you write. Neither tool knows your voice, so treat every highlight and suggested rewrite as a question, not an order. Hemingway itself says to focus on the worst offenders rather than fixing every highlight.

How the costs compare

Hemingway Editor Plus starts at $8.33 a month billed yearly ($100), and Grammarly Pro starts at $12 a month. Hemingway's 2-week trial needs no credit card. If you only want readability scores and don't need AI rewrites, the free checker or the $19.99 one-time desktop app may be all you need.

Our pick

Start with both free versions. Upgrade to Grammarly Pro if you want help rewriting sentences in every app you write in. Upgrade to Hemingway Editor Plus only if long, hard-to-read sentences are your biggest problem and you want AI fixes for them. Most Substack writers won't need either paid plan. For more options, see the Editing & Proofreading category, including Grammarly vs ProWritingAid.

Our verdicts

  • Hemingway Editor: The free web version does the core job (highlighting long and passive sentences). Most writers don't need Plus.
  • Grammarly: The free browser extension is worth installing. Pro's rewrites can flatten your voice, so accept suggestions selectively.
