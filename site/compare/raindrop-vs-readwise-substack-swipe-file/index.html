Raindrop.io vs Readwise for your Substack swipe file and research

Checked 2026-11-23 on the vendors' sites and Substack's help center · Independent, no affiliate relationship with either tool

The short answer: Pick Raindrop.io if you want a free place to save links you'll come back to: headlines you liked, post structures to borrow, sources for your next issue. Its free plan has unlimited bookmarks and collections. Pick Readwise ($9.99/mo billed yearly for Reader + Highlights, 30-day free trial) if you read a lot of books and articles and want your highlights collected, resurfaced and searchable when you write.

The real difference

Raindrop.io is a filing cabinet for links: you save something in a second and find it later by collection or tag. Readwise is built around reading: you read inside Reader, highlight what matters, and it brings those highlights back to you. A swipe file only needs the first; a research habit built on books and long articles gets more from the second.

A free swipe file setup

In Raindrop.io, make one collection per purpose: headlines that made you click, post structures you want to borrow, and research links for your next issue. Full-text search of saved pages is a Pro feature, so add a short note to each bookmark saying why you saved it. That note is what you'll search for later.

When Readwise earns its price

If you write essays from books you've read, Readwise pulls in your Kindle and other highlights and resurfaces them in a Daily Review, so quotes and ideas come back to you while you're drafting. Use the 30-day trial on a real post before you commit to a yearly plan.

Our pick

Start with Raindrop.io's free plan for a swipe file and link library. Add Readwise only if reading books and long articles is the main input to your writing. If you're also planning posts, see Notion vs Trello for an editorial calendar.

Our verdicts

  • Raindrop.io: A bookmark manager with a free plan that most writers will never outgrow: make one collection per purpose (headlines, structures, sources for the next issue) and save from the browser extension or phone. The catch: searching the full text of saved pages is a Pro feature, so add a quick note to each bookmark about why you saved it.
  • Readwise: A strong research engine for essayists and curators. Its Reader app is included with Full.
