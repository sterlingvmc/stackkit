Obsidian vs TK for capturing ideas and drafting Substack posts

Checked 2026-11-10 on the vendors' sites and Substack's help center · Independent, no affiliate relationship with either tool

The short answer: Pick Obsidian if you want your idea bank to live on your own computer as plain Markdown files, free with no account. Try TK if you want an AI assistant you can call mid-sentence and quick headline or editorial feedback, and you're fine with a brand-new, cloud-stored app tied to a Medium account.

What this choice is really about

Neither tool publishes to Substack. Both are places to collect ideas and draft before you paste into the Substack editor. The difference is ownership versus assistance: Obsidian gives you files you control forever, while TK gives you an AI helper and feedback inside the draft in exchange for keeping your notes in Medium's cloud.

When TK is worth a try

If you stall on headlines or want a second opinion on a draft without opening a separate chatbot, TK's @TK prompt and feedback skills are the draw. It's also a decent fit if you already have a Medium account. It's days old, though, so treat it as a workshop rather than the only home for your archive, and export your notes to Markdown now and then.

When Obsidian is the safer bet

If your idea bank is years of research, quotes and half-finished posts, Obsidian's local Markdown files mean no company can lock you out or change the terms. You give up built-in AI, but you lose nothing if you switch tools later.

Our pick

Obsidian for the long-term archive. If you're curious about AI feedback that doesn't write for you, try TK alongside it with a few drafts. TK can import an Obsidian vault, so testing it doesn't mean starting over.

Our verdicts

  • Obsidian: Great for writers who think in linked notes. Your files stay local in Markdown.
  • TK: Medium's new private notebook (opened to all Medium accounts Nov 9, 2026): type @TK mid-sentence to ask a question or pull in an old note, get editorial and headline feedback, and import Bear or Obsidian vaults. It can also connect to your own AI agent through its MCP server or a Claude skill. Brand new, web and mobile only for now (no desktop app), notes are cloud-stored and not encrypted, Medium hasn't said which AI model it uses, and it has no Substack integration. Export to Markdown if you try it.
