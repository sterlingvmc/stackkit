Canva vs Kittl for Substack images

Checked 2026-11-13 on the vendors' sites and Substack's help center · Independent, no affiliate relationship with either tool

The short answer: Pick Canva if you want the simplest free way to make Substack header, preview and Notes images: it's the default for newsletter writers and Pro is US$144/yr. Pick Kittl if typography-heavy, stylized graphics are part of your brand, but its free-plan license terms are currently contradictory, so a paid or business Substack should budget for Pro (listed at $15/mo or $144/yr, though Kittl's live cards have also shown $19/mo or $14/mo billed yearly).

What size to make your Substack images

Substack's help center recommends a social/post preview image of at least 1200 x 630 px, and says 14:10 is the aspect ratio for preview images. Post thumbnails use 3:2. A logo should be at least 256 x 256 px with a transparent background, an email banner around 1100 x 220 px, and a cover image at least 600 x 600 px. Keep the important part of a preview image near the center so it survives cropping on mobile. Both tools let you set a custom canvas size, so set it once and save it as a template.

The license question most writers miss

If your Substack has paid subscriptions, sponsors, or promotes a business, your images are arguably commercial use. Kittl's pricing FAQ says its free plan is for personal use only, with no commercial rights, and that commercial rights start on Pro. But as of November 13, 2026, the Free card on the same pricing page has been seen saying 'Full commercial license'. Until Kittl resolves that, treat Kittl free as fine for a hobby newsletter and get written confirmation (or use Pro) for a paid one. Whatever tool you use, read its license before you build a business on it.

Price for a solo writer

Paid yearly, Canva Pro and Kittl Pro cost about the same: US$144 a year each, going by Kittl's plan data (its live cards have also shown $14/mo billed yearly, about $168). The difference is what you get. Canva Pro adds a large premium template and asset library on top of a free plan most writers never outgrow. Kittl Pro is mostly worth it for its type tools, premium templates and commercial license.

Our pick

For most Substack writers, Canva: the free plan covers header, preview and Notes images, and you can make a full post image in minutes. Choose Kittl if distinctive typography is a core part of your publication's look and you're willing to pay for Pro once your Substack makes money.

Our verdicts

  • Canva: The default design tool for newsletter writers. The free plan covers most needs.
  • Kittl: A stylish Canva alternative with great type tools. Its license terms are currently inconsistent: the pricing FAQ says the free plan is personal use only (no commercial rights), while the live Free pricing card has been seen saying 'Full commercial license'. If your Substack earns money, assume you need Pro (which includes a commercial license) until Kittl clarifies.
