Substack tool comparisons

Head-to-head comparisons of tools for Substack writers, checked against each tool's own pricing and feature pages. Each one ends with a plain recommendation.

  • Datawrapper vs Flourish for Substack
    Pick Datawrapper if you want interactive charts inside your Substack posts: it's the only one of the two that Substack embeds natively, and its free plan is unlimited. Pick Flourish only if you need animated or scrollytelling visuals and are happy to link out to them from a static image.
  • Substack Insights vs NoteStack for Substack
    Pick Substack Insights if you just want to see which posts and Notes work and when to publish: it's free under 1,000 subscribers and $9.99 one-time above that, and it only reads your data. Pick NoteStack if Notes are your main growth channel and you also want subscriber-conversion rankings, a scheduler and optional AI drafting, from $8/mo.
  • Buffer vs Typefully for Substack
    Both free plans schedule Substack Notes, so you can try either at no cost. Pick Buffer if Notes are one channel among many (including Instagram, TikTok or Facebook) or you want to see which Notes brought subscribers. Pick Typefully if you write for X or LinkedIn first and want a writing-focused editor. If you only post Notes, Substack's free built-in scheduler may be all you need.
  • Claude vs Sudowrite for Substack
    Pick Sudowrite if your Substack is serialized fiction and you want a tool built around story work: its plans include style matching and continuity across a series, with a free trial and no card. Pick Claude if you write essays alongside fiction or mainly want feedback on long drafts: it has a free plan and Pro is $20/mo ($17/mo billed annually). Either way, keep the published prose your own.
  • Grammarly vs ProWritingAid for Substack
    Pick Grammarly if you want quick, everywhere proofreading: its free browser extension checks spelling and grammar as you type on the web, and Pro (from $12/mo) adds full-sentence rewrites and tone adjustments. Pick ProWritingAid if you write long essays or serialized fiction and want deep style reports: Premium is $10/mo billed yearly ($120) or $30 monthly. Both browser extensions work on web pages, so either can check drafts in the Substack editor.
  • ChatGPT vs Perplexity for Substack
    Pick Perplexity when you need facts you can check: every answer comes with citations, and the free plan covers light research. Pick ChatGPT when you want a brainstorming and editing partner for angles, outlines and headlines; its free plan includes unlimited everyday text chats, Go is $8/mo and Plus is $20/mo. Perplexity Pro is $20/mo, or $17/mo billed annually.
  • PlotStack vs Substack Insights for Substack
    Both are read-only Chrome extensions that work from your logged-in Substack session and keep data in your browser. Pick Substack Insights for a quick, simple answer to what works and when to publish (free under 1,000 subscribers, $9.99 one-time above). Pick PlotStack if you want everything in one detailed dashboard, including growth sources, retention and per-Note signups, at no cost, and don't mind a very new one-person tool that doesn't support custom domains yet.
  • Hemingway Editor vs Grammarly for Substack
    They do different jobs, and both free versions are worth using. Grammarly catches spelling, grammar and tone mistakes as you type, including in the Substack editor through its browser extension. Hemingway Editor's free readability checker shows your reading grade level and highlights hard-to-read sentences, which helps with dense posts. If you only pay for one, Grammarly Pro (from $12/mo) suits writers who want sentence rewrites everywhere they write; Hemingway Editor Plus (from $8.33/mo billed yearly) suits writers whose main problem is long, tangled sentences.
  • Orbit vs StackContacts Studio for Substack
    Both help you see who's waiting on a reply and who you've lost touch with on Substack. Pick Orbit ($12/mo or $120/yr via a paid subscription to its maker's Substack) if your relationships live on Substack and you want a browser dashboard, Claude or ChatGPT questions, and a tool that never writes or sends anything for you. Pick StackContacts Studio ($99 one-time) if you also sell on Gumroad, keep a Kit list or run a Skool community and want everything matched on your own computer, with Claude drafting replies and Notes that go out only after you approve them.
  • Notion vs Trello for Substack
    Both free plans are enough for one writer. Pick Trello if you just want a simple board where each post card moves from Idea to Drafting to Scheduled to Published. Pick Notion if you also want an idea bank, research notes, a sponsor list and a calendar view in one place, and don't mind spending an hour setting it up.
  • Gumroad vs Ko-fi for Substack
    Pick Ko-fi if you mainly want a tip jar next to your Substack: one-off tips are fee-free on Ko-fi Free (you pay only PayPal or Stripe fees). Pick Gumroad if you're selling an ebook, template or guide and want sales tax handled for you; it costs more per sale but acts as merchant of record.
