Quick Viral Notes vs Narrareach for turning posts into Notes

Checked 2026-11-18 on the vendors' sites and Substack's help center · Independent, no affiliate relationship with either tool

The short answer: Both turn a published Substack post into a batch of Notes. Pick Quick Viral Notes ($7/mo or $65/yr) if that's all you want: paste a URL, get formula-based Notes drafts, schedule them from a Chrome extension. Pick Narrareach (from $19.99/mo) if you also want subscriber attribution, a voice profile and cross-posting to Medium and LinkedIn, with Notes handed to Substack's own scheduler.

The real difference

Quick Viral Notes does one job cheaply: it turns an article into draft Notes. Narrareach is a full publishing desk where article-to-Notes is one feature among scheduling, analytics, cross-posting and AI replies. If you only post on Substack and already like Substack's built-in Notes scheduler, the cheaper, narrower tool may be all you need.

Watch the formula feel

Both tools write from patterns: Quick Viral Notes from named viral formulas, Narrareach from your voice profile. Regular readers notice when every Note opens the same way. Edit each draft so it sounds like you, and mix formula Notes with ones you write from scratch.

How they publish

Quick Viral Notes' scheduler extension posts from your browser at the slot time, so your computer and Chrome need to be on. Narrareach hands Notes to Substack's native scheduler. Either way, you can skip the scheduler and paste Notes into Substack's own free scheduler. See every option on our Notes schedulers page.

Our pick

Writers on a tight budget who just want a head start on Notes from each post: Quick Viral Notes, starting with the free 10 credits. Writers who want to know which Notes bring subscribers and also publish to Medium or LinkedIn: Narrareach. Try each free trial on one recent post before paying.

Our verdicts

  • Quick Viral Notes: Inexpensive and focused: paste a Substack post URL, it pulls out the ideas and drafts Notes from named formulas, and keeps every idea in a reusable Knowledge Bank. A small tool from Jenny Ouyang of the Build to Launch newsletter, newly promoted on launch directories in Nov 2026 (its scheduler extension has a single Chrome Web Store rating). Formula-driven Notes can read alike, so edit them into your own voice. Its scheduler is a separate Chrome extension that posts through your logged-in Substack session, and your browser must be running at the scheduled time.
  • Narrareach: Good if distribution beyond Substack is your bottleneck. Hands Notes to Substack's native scheduler rather than posting from a stored session.
