Orbit vs the Substack Engagement Plugin

Checked 2026-11-15 on the vendors' sites and Substack's help center · Independent, no affiliate relationship with either tool

The short answer: Both help you keep up with conversations on Substack, but they take opposite approaches. Pick Orbit ($12/mo or $120/yr via its maker's paid Substack) if you want to remember who you've talked to, find missed replies and see which Notes bring subscribers, while writing every word yourself. Pick the Substack Engagement Plugin ($67 one-time) if your bottleneck is time to write comments and replies, and you're comfortable approving AI drafts in your voice.

The real difference

Orbit's maker built it on the idea that you should do the talking: it helps you remember and decide who to talk to, then gets out of the way. The Engagement Plugin is built to save the writing time itself: it drafts the comment, reply or Note and you approve, edit or skip it. Neither is wrong, but they suit different writers.

The voice question

Readers on Substack notice comments that sound generic. If you use the plugin, treat drafts as a starting point and edit them. If your voice is the reason people follow you, Orbit's approach (or a free spreadsheet) keeps every word yours.

How the costs compare

Orbit is $120 over a year paid annually and also gets you its maker's paid newsletter. The plugin is $67 once, but you need the Claude or ChatGPT desktop app, and it reads Substack best with Claude in Chrome or ChatGPT's browser tools. Neither offers a free trial, so read the sales pages before you buy.

Our pick

Writers who want to stay close to people and write everything themselves: Orbit. Busy writers who will actually edit AI drafts before approving them: the Substack Engagement Plugin. If you want a free, self-hosted relationship tracker and are technical, look at Circles in the Analytics category.

Our verdicts

  • Orbit: A thoughtful relationship tracker for Substack that never writes or replies for you, and you can ask it questions from Claude or ChatGPT. It works through a Chrome extension running alongside your logged-in Substack session (DMs off by default), and there's no way to try it before paying.
  • Substack Engagement Plugin: A plugin for Claude or ChatGPT desktop that drafts replies to your comments, comments on other writers' Notes, Notes of your own, restack lines and recommendation-swap pitches, plus a weekly scorecard ranked by subscribers. Every draft waits in an approval list; if you choose, it posts approved drafts one at a time up to a daily limit, and it never sends cold DMs. The upside is speed; the risk is sounding like everyone else using AI, so edit before you approve. The maker's own results (replies up 212% in a month) are self-reported and, as she says, not typical. It works best with the Claude in Chrome extension or ChatGPT's browser tools reading your Substack; otherwise you paste or screenshot.
