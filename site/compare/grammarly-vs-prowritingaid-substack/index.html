Grammarly vs ProWritingAid for Substack writers

Checked 2026-10-28 on the vendors' sites and Substack's help center · Independent, no affiliate relationship with either tool

The short answer: Pick Grammarly if you want quick, everywhere proofreading: its free browser extension checks spelling and grammar as you type on the web, and Pro (from $12/mo) adds full-sentence rewrites and tone adjustments. Pick ProWritingAid if you write long essays or serialized fiction and want deep style reports: Premium is $10/mo billed yearly ($120) or $30 monthly. Both browser extensions work on web pages, so either can check drafts in the Substack editor.

Start free with either

Most Substack writers only need a second pair of eyes for typos before hitting publish. Both free plans catch spelling and grammar. Grammarly's free extension does it as you type; ProWritingAid's free reports cap you at 500 words at a time, which is fine for a Note but tight for a full post.

Essays and fiction: reports beat rewrites

If your posts run long, ProWritingAid's reports on repetition, sentence length, pacing, dialogue tags and readability show patterns across a whole draft. That's more useful for a weekly essay or chapter than one-sentence fixes. Its fiction tools (Chapter Critique, Story Canvas) only matter if you publish stories.

Keep your voice

Grammarly Pro's full-sentence rewrites and tone adjustments can make every post sound the same. Readers subscribe for how you write, so accept suggestions one at a time and skip the ones that don't sound like you. The same goes for ProWritingAid's Rephrases.

How the costs compare

Paying monthly, Grammarly Pro starts at $12 against ProWritingAid Premium's $30. Paying yearly, ProWritingAid Premium works out to $10 a month ($120). Check Grammarly's checkout for its billing options, since its plans page shows the Pro price without a yearly total.

Our pick

For most Substack writers, start with Grammarly's free extension for everyday proofreading. Add ProWritingAid Premium (billed yearly) if you publish long essays or serialized fiction and want a deeper edit before each post. For other options, including the free Hemingway web editor and LanguageTool, see the Editing & Proofreading category.

Our verdicts

  • Grammarly: The free browser extension is worth installing. Pro's rewrites can flatten your voice, so accept suggestions selectively.
  • ProWritingAid: More thorough reports than Grammarly. Worth trying if you write long essays or serialized fiction.
