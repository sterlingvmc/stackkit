Notion vs Trello for planning your Substack

Checked 2026-11-03 on the vendors' sites and Substack's help center · Independent, no affiliate relationship with either tool

The short answer: Both free plans are enough for one writer. Pick Trello if you just want a simple board where each post card moves from Idea to Drafting to Scheduled to Published. Pick Notion if you also want an idea bank, research notes, a sponsor list and a calendar view in one place, and don't mind spending an hour setting it up.

What a Substack writer actually needs

Most writers need three things: somewhere to dump ideas, a way to see what's publishing this week, and a record of what went out. Either tool does all three for free. The choice is about how much structure you want.

When Trello is enough

If you publish once or twice a week, a Trello board with four lists (Ideas, Drafting, Scheduled, Published) is hard to beat. Put the publish date on each card and you can see the next month at a glance.

When Notion pays off

If you run sponsorships, a podcast or a paid tier alongside your posts, Notion lets you link a post to its sponsor, its research notes and its Notes promotion in one database. That's more setup, but it saves hunting across apps later.

Our pick

Start with Trello's free plan if you want to be planning in five minutes. Choose Notion's free plan if you want one place for your whole newsletter business. Neither connects to Substack directly, so you'll still schedule posts inside Substack. See the Planning category for other options, like Obsidian.

Our verdicts

  • Notion: The most flexible planning hub for newsletter writers. The free plan covers solo use.
  • Trello: Simpler than Notion if you only need a board. Free is plenty for one writer.
