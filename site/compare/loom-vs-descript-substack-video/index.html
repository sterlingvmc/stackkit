Loom vs Descript for recording Substack video

Checked 2026-11-27 on the vendors' sites and Substack's help center · Independent, no affiliate relationship with either tool

The short answer: Try Substack's own Recording Studio first: it's free, records your screen and camera, and the video lands straight in your post. Pick Loom for quick screen walkthroughs you share as a link (free plan: 5-minute recordings, 25 videos). Pick Descript if you want to edit the video before it goes on Substack: you cut it by editing the transcript, and its free plan exports a watermarked 720p file you can upload.

Start with Substack's own recorder

Substack can record video for you. Create a video post (or add a video to an article with the camera icon), click Record, and you're in the Substack Recording Studio, which can share your screen, host guests and add a logo. The video lands in your draft with an AI transcript, and Substack takes uploads up to a recommended 20 GB in MP4, MOV and other common formats. If you only need to talk to camera or walk through your screen, you may not need either tool.

When Loom fits

Loom is the quickest way to record a short screen walkthrough and share it as a link: show readers how you set up a tool, answer one subscriber's question with a video, or give a sponsor a tour. The free plan stops each recording at 5 minutes and keeps 25 videos. Because its pricing page puts downloading under Business, Loom works best when a link is all you need, not a file for Substack.

When Descript fits

If the video is the post, Descript gives you more control: delete words from the transcript to cut them from the video, remove filler words, and clean up the sound before you upload the file to Substack. The free plan is fine for testing, but its 720p exports carry a watermark, so plan on Hobbyist if you publish video regularly.

Our pick

Use Substack's Recording Studio for straightforward video posts. Add Loom's free plan if you often answer questions with a short screen recording. Choose Descript when you want an edited, cleaned-up video or podcast on Substack.

Our verdicts

  • Loom: Fast for a short how-to or a walkthrough of a tool you're writing about: record your screen with your face in a bubble and share a link. For a video that lives on Substack, start with Substack's own free Recording Studio (it records your screen too). On Loom's free plan recordings stop at 5 minutes and its pricing page lists uploading and downloading videos as a Business feature, so getting a file out to upload to Substack means paying.
  • Descript: The easiest way for writers to edit audio and video. A natural fit for Substack podcasts.
