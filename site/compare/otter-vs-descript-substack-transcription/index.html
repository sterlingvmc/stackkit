Otter.ai vs Descript for transcribing interviews and voice drafts

Checked 2026-11-24 on the vendors' sites and Substack's help center · Independent, no affiliate relationship with either tool

The short answer: Pick Otter.ai if you mainly need text: interview transcripts to quote from or a dictated first draft. Its free plan gives 300 transcription minutes a month, capped at 30 minutes per conversation. Pick Descript if the recording itself is going out as a Substack podcast or video, because you edit the audio by editing the transcript. Its free plan covers 60 media minutes a month.

Which job do you actually have?

Most writers who search for transcription want one of two things. Either you need words on a page (an interview you'll quote, or a draft you talked through on a walk), or you need a finished recording for a Substack podcast or video post. Otter.ai is built for the first, Descript for the second. Both transcribe, so the question is what happens after the transcript.

Interviews and quotes

Record the call or conversation in the Otter app and you get a speaker-labeled transcript you can search and copy from. On the free plan, keep each recording under 30 minutes and record live rather than importing files, since Basic allows only 3 imports in total. Always check a quote against the audio before it goes in your post.

Talking through a first draft

If you just want to dictate, try free Google Docs voice typing first (Tools, then Voice typing, in Chrome, Edge or Safari). Otter.ai earns its place when you'd rather ramble into your phone away from the desk and clean up the text later.

Publishing audio on Substack

If the recording is the post, Descript is the better fit: delete a sentence from the transcript and it's cut from the audio, and its AI tools remove filler words and background noise. For cleaning up a single voiceover for free, Adobe Podcast's Enhance Speech is also worth a look.

Our pick

Use Otter.ai's free plan for interview transcripts and spoken drafts. Use Descript's free plan if you're testing a Substack podcast, and move to Hobbyist once you regularly go past an hour of recordings a month.

Our verdicts

  • Otter.ai: Good at one job a writer actually has: turning a recorded interview or a voice memo into searchable text with speakers labeled. The free plan's 30-minute cap per conversation and 3 lifetime file imports make it a trial for anything longer than short calls; record live in the Otter app to avoid the import limit. Much of the product now centers on AI meeting notes, which most writers can ignore. Check quotes against the audio before you publish them.
  • Descript: The easiest way for writers to edit audio and video. A natural fit for Substack podcasts.
