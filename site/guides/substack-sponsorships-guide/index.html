Substack sponsorships guide: how to land your first newsletter sponsor (2026)

Updated 2026-11-14 · Rules checked against Substack's help center and Publisher Agreement; marketplace fees checked on each vendor's own pricing page · Independent, not affiliated with Substack Inc.

The short version: Substack lets you run sponsors in your newsletter, but it doesn't sell ads for you unless you qualify for its invite-style sponsorships program (Bestsellers or 100+ paid subscribers). Below that, list your own sponsor slots on a free marketplace like Hecto, browse deals on Swapstack, or earn from paid recommendations on SparkLoop. Big ad networks such as Paved have minimums most small Substacks don't meet yet. Whatever you run, label it clearly.

1. Is it allowed? What Substack's rules say

  • Sponsored content is allowed. Substack's help center says its own business model relies on subscriptions, not advertisers, but "you're welcome to host sponsored content" and it has no policy prohibiting it.
  • You must disclose it. The Publisher Agreement (updated November 2, 2026, with new sponsored-content terms) says publishers paid by a sponsor must follow the laws on disclosing and labeling sponsored, paid or endorsed content wherever their content is available, and that Substack may require a specific format, placement or wording.
  • Your publication can't be mainly ads. Substack's Content Guidelines don't permit publications whose primary purpose is advertising external products, driving traffic to third-party sites or distributing offers and promotions.
  • No built-in ad tools. Substack's help center says it doesn't build functionality to support affiliate links or advertising, so you place and track sponsor sections yourself.

2. Substack's own sponsorships program (Creator Kits)

Substack runs a native sponsorships program. According to its help center, Creator Kits are available to all Bestseller publications or those with at least 100 paid subscribers; if you qualify, you'll see a Sponsorships tab in your dashboard.

  • Build it: go to your publication's Home, open Sponsorships in the left sidebar (Bestsellers may also see "Go to Sponsorships" in the announcements banner) and click Build creator kit.
  • What's in it: a description, the stats you choose to show (such as subscriber count, open rate and active readers), audience location and interests, past brand partners, testimonials, and up to ten sponsorship formats with a minimum spend.
  • Private by default: Creator Kits aren't public. Only brands in the program that Substack's team connects you with can see them, and for now Substack handles all matchmaking by email.
  • Tell them what you won't take: a private "Not interested" section lets you rule out brands or formats.

It's optional and free to set up. If you qualify, turn it on, but don't wait on it: it's matchmaking, not a marketplace you control.

3. Below the threshold: sponsor marketplaces that don't ask for a minimum

None of these list a subscriber minimum on their pricing pages:

  • Hecto: you publish your own sponsor packages with upfront prices and a checkout link. No monthly fee; Hecto takes 5% of successful sales, plus Stripe's fees. Best if you want to set your price and let sponsors buy without back-and-forth.
  • Swapstack: free for publishers. You browse pay-per-click and affiliate opportunities, and Swapstack takes 10% on invite-only campaigns from its managed service. Best for seeing what's available in your niche with no upfront pricing work.
  • SparkLoop: not a sponsor marketplace, but its free Partner Network pays you to recommend other newsletters to new subscribers. For many small newsletters it's the first "sponsor-like" money.

We compare the first two side by side on the Hecto vs Swapstack page.

4. When the big ad networks open up

Paved describes itself as the world's largest newsletter ad marketplace. Its publisher account is free, but flat-fee scheduled ads need at least 50,000 subscribers, and pay-per-click ads need at least 1 million monthly impressions. Bookmark it for later; it isn't where most Substacks find their first sponsor.

5. Pitch sponsors directly

Marketplaces are optional. Many first sponsors come from a direct email to a company your readers already use. A simple process:

  1. List five to ten brands you've already mentioned or recommended in your posts.
  2. Write a one-page media kit: who reads you, your subscriber count and open rate, two or three posts that represent you, and one or two formats with a price. Substack's Creator Kit fields are a good template even if you don't qualify yet.
  3. Send a short email to their marketing or partnerships contact with a specific offer, such as one mention in a specific issue on a specific date.
  4. Deliver and report: after the issue goes out, send the sponsor the clicks from their link. A free link shortener or UTM tags make this easy; see the analytics tools.

6. How to run a sponsor section readers don't mind

  • Label it plainly (for example, "Sponsored by…" at the top of the section). Substack's agreement requires disclosure, and readers trust labeled sponsors more than hidden ones.
  • One sponsor per issue, in the same spot each time, so readers know where it is and can skip it.
  • Only take sponsors you'd recommend without being paid. Your recommendations are the asset you're selling.
  • Keep your paid subscribers in mind. Some writers keep paid-only posts ad-free as a perk; that's a choice, not a Substack rule.

7. Other ways to earn beyond paid subscriptions

If sponsors aren't a fit yet, sell something of your own with Gumroad (no monthly fee; it collects sales tax for you as merchant of record) or add a tip jar with Ko-fi (one-off tips are fee-free on Ko-fi Free). Fees compared on the Gumroad vs Ko-fi page.

Tools mentioned in this guide

  • Hecto: Selling sponsorship slots with transparent, upfront prices. Free for newsletter creators (no monthly fee; shown as £0/month); Hecto takes 5% of successful sponsorship sales, plus Stripe's payment fees.
  • Swapstack: Finding sponsors and affiliate deals for small and mid-sized newsletters. Free for publishers (Swapstack takes 10% on invite-only campaigns from its managed service). Paid publisher plans (Professional $30/mo, Enterprise $200/mo) are listed as coming soon.
  • Paved: Established newsletters looking for sponsor demand. Free publisher account, no subscription. Flat-fee scheduled ads need at least 50k subscribers; pay-per-click native ads need at least 1M monthly impressions.
  • SparkLoop: Getting paid recommendations and buying cross-newsletter referrals. Free to join the Partner Network and Upscribe recommendation widget (SparkLoop takes a share of paid-recommendation earnings). Paying other newsletters to recommend you is a separate paid program.
  • Gumroad: Selling ebooks, templates and guides alongside your newsletter. No monthly fee; 10% + $0.50 per transaction on direct sales (profile or direct links); 30% on sales through Gumroad Discover.
  • Ko-fi: One-off tips from readers who won't commit to a paid subscription. Free to join, no monthly cost. Ko-fi Free: 0% on one-off tips and Goals, 5% on monthly tips, memberships, shop and commissions. Standard: 5% on everything, plus extras like supporter-only content and scheduled posts. PayPal/Stripe fees (usually around 3% + $0.30) apply on top. Ko-fi Gold (0% on everything for a fixed monthly fee) is still offered; price not listed on its page.
