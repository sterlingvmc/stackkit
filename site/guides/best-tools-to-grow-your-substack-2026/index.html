Best tools to grow your Substack in 2026

Updated 2026-10-29 · Every tool and price below was checked on the vendor's site · 30 tools

Substack gives you a lot out of the box: hosting, email, payments, recommendations and Notes. So the question isn't "what do I need?" but "what fills the gaps that limit my growth?" This guide walks through those gaps in the order most writers hit them, recommends specific tools for each, and is upfront about the trade-offs.

The short version: start free (Google Analytics, Canva, Grammarly, Notion). Join a recommendation network early (SparkLoop). Treat Notes as your main discovery channel and measure which Notes convert (Subshack is free, WriteStack is the full suite). Add revenue analytics (Byline OS) once paid subscribers matter.

1. Start with the free basics

Before paying for anything, set up the free foundations. They cover most of what a new publication needs.

  • Google Analytics: Free and worth connecting on day one for referral sources. Overkill for most writers beyond the acquisition reports. Pricing: Free.
  • Canva: The default design tool for newsletter writers. The free plan covers most needs. Pricing: Free; Pro US$144/yr for one person (before tax).
  • Grammarly: The free browser extension is worth installing. Pro's rewrites can flatten your voice, so accept suggestions selectively. Pricing: Free (includes 100 AI prompts/mo); Pro from $12/mo with a free trial (2,000 AI prompts/mo, full-sentence rewrites, tone adjustments, plagiarism and AI-text detection).
  • Hemingway Editor: The free web version does the core job (highlighting long and passive sentences). Most writers don't need Plus. Pricing: Free web editor; Editor Plus (AI rewrites) Individual 5K $8.33/mo billed annually ($100/yr) or Individual 10K $12.50/mo billed annually ($150/yr); desktop app $19.99 one-time (no AI features).
  • Notion: The most flexible planning hub for newsletter writers. The free plan covers solo use. Pricing: Free; Plus $10/seat/mo.
  • Unsplash: The go-to free photo library. Popular images show up everywhere, so crop or edit them to stand out. Pricing: Free to use under the Unsplash License.

2. Get subscribers from other newsletters

Recommendations are one of the most reliable growth channels for newsletters. If you also have a personal site, make the signup form there worth using.

  • SparkLoop: The best-known newsletter recommendation network. Earning from recommendations is low-risk; paying for referrals only makes sense once you know what a subscriber is worth to you. Pricing: Free to join the Partner Network and Upscribe recommendation widget (SparkLoop takes a share of paid-recommendation earnings). Paying other newsletters to recommend you is a separate paid program.
  • Supascribe: Fills a real gap: Substack's native embed is fixed-width and unstyled. Independent (not affiliated with Substack), and only useful if you drive traffic from a site you control. Pricing: Free (2 basic embeds, first 1,000 views, first 100 new subscribers); Starter $9/mo, Growth $20/mo, Pro $249/mo (all billed yearly); paid plans add $2 per extra 10,000 views or 100 subscribers.

3. Win on Substack Notes

Notes is where Substack readers discover new writers. Substack now schedules single Notes natively; the tools below add batch queues, cross-posting and, most importantly, attribution: which Notes actually bring subscribers. For every scheduler side by side, see Substack Notes schedulers compared.

  • Buffer: Reliable and affordable, and its free plan includes Substack Notes scheduling. The best default if you post to several networks. Pricing: Free (up to 3 channels, 10 scheduled posts per channel at a time); Essentials $5/channel/mo billed yearly ($6 monthly); Team $10/channel/mo billed yearly ($12 monthly).
  • WriteStack: The most complete Substack-only Notes suite. Pricey for a beginner, and publishing runs through a Chromium browser extension acting in your logged-in session. Pricing: Hobbyist $23.99/mo ($239.88/yr); Standard $32.99/mo ($299.88/yr); Enterprise $99.99/mo ($959.88/yr); 7-day free trial.
  • Narrareach: Good if distribution beyond Substack is your bottleneck. Hands Notes to Substack's native scheduler rather than posting from a stored session. Pricing: Audience Builder $19.99/mo ($199/yr); Revenue Writer $39/mo ($390/yr); Full Agentic Mode $89/mo ($890/yr). Every plan starts with a 7-day free trial, no card required.
  • StackSweller: Does one job (a Notes queue that runs with your laptop closed). Be aware it works by holding your Substack session, and Substack now schedules single Notes natively. Pricing: Pro $39/mo, or $25/mo billed yearly ($250/yr).
  • Subshack: A free, measurement-first browser extension (Chrome and Firefox) that never posts Notes or articles for you. Optional welcome-message sending is off unless you turn it on, with daily limits. New and small, so expect rough edges. Pricing: Free (Subshack 1.0), including a no-signup public profile audit.

4. Cross-post where your readers already are

Pick one or two networks where your audience lives and repurpose each post there, rather than spreading yourself across all of them.

  • Typefully: A polished writing-first scheduler that now publishes and schedules Substack Notes on every plan, including free. It handles Notes only (no long-form posts, no video in Notes), and its paid analytics are for X, so it suits writers who start on X or LinkedIn more than Notes-first writers. Pricing: Free (10 posts/mo, 1 social set; Substack Notes included); Creator $99/yr (annual billing only); Business $18 per social set/mo billed yearly.
  • Taplio: Effective for LinkedIn but expensive. Only worth it if LinkedIn is a primary acquisition channel. Pricing: Starter $39/mo; Growth $69/mo; Pro $199/mo; 7-day free trial.
  • Hypefury: Hypefury no longer supports X, so it's now a scheduler for Bluesky, Threads, LinkedIn, Instagram and TikTok, with auto first comments (Autoplugs) and an engagement builder. Substack Notes isn't on its channel list, so pair it with Substack's own scheduler. Pricing: Flexible $6/mo for 1 channel (plus $6/mo per extra channel); Full $19/mo for all channels; 7-day free trial, no card. No free plan.
  • Repurpose.io: Saves real time if you make video or audio. Pure text writers don't need it. Pricing: Starter $35/mo; Pro $79/mo ($790/yr); Agency $179/mo ($1,790/yr); free trial of 14 days or 10 published videos, whichever comes first.

5. Measure what converts to paid

Opens and likes aren't revenue. Once you have paid subscribers, track which posts drive upgrades and where churn comes from.

  • Byline OS: Promising revenue-first analytics built specifically for Substack. It is a beta product; live sync uses your Substack session cookie and undocumented internal APIs, so the CSV-upload mode is the safer start. Pricing: Starter free (CSV import, 30-day retention); Growth $19/mo; Scale $49/mo. 14-day trial. Currently in private beta.
  • Subshack: A free, measurement-first browser extension (Chrome and Firefox) that never posts Notes or articles for you. Optional welcome-message sending is off unless you turn it on, with daily limits. New and small, so expect rough edges. Pricing: Free (Subshack 1.0), including a no-signup public profile audit.
  • Bitly: Handy for knowing which channel sends readers. The free tier is limited; many writers can get by with UTM tags plus Google Analytics. Pricing: Free (with ads; 5 links and 2 QR Codes a month); Core $10/mo (annual only, $120/yr); Growth $29/mo ($348/yr); Premium $199/mo ($2,388/yr).
  • Plausible: Excellent lightweight analytics, but you can't install it on a substack.com publication. Use it on a personal site that funnels readers to Substack. Pricing: From $9/mo (Starter, 10k monthly pageviews); Growth $14/mo; Business $19/mo. Free trial.

6. Add audio and video

Substack supports podcasts and video. Audio versions and short clips can reach people who won't read a 2,000-word post.

  • Descript: The easiest way for writers to edit audio and video. A natural fit for Substack podcasts. Pricing: Free; Hobbyist $16/mo billed annually ($24 monthly); Creator $24/mo annually ($35 monthly).
  • ElevenLabs: Best-in-class AI voice. Tell listeners when a voice is synthetic. Pricing: Free (10k credits/mo); Starter $6/mo; Creator $22/mo; higher tiers available.
  • Riverside: Records each guest locally, so remote interviews sound studio-grade. Ideal for interview-driven newsletters. Pricing: Free (720p, Riverside watermark, 2 one-off hours of separate-track recording); Pro $24/mo billed annually ($288/yr) or $29 monthly; Grow $34/mo billed annually.
  • OpusClip: Fast way to get shorts from long recordings. Review the AI's clip choices before posting. Pricing: Free (60 credits/mo); Starter $15/mo (monthly only, 150 credits); Pro $29/mo or $14.50/mo billed annually.
  • Adobe Podcast: Enhance Speech is impressive on the free tier. Check Adobe's current plans before relying on premium features. Pricing: Free tier; premium features require a paid Adobe plan (check site for current offer).

7. Earn beyond subscriptions

Sponsorships, digital products and tips can out-earn paid subscriptions for some niches, and they don't put your writing behind a paywall.

  • Hecto: A straightforward sponsorship marketplace: you publish ad packages with upfront prices and a checkout link. Listing is free, but demand varies by niche. Pricing: Free for newsletter creators (no monthly fee); Hecto takes 5% of successful sponsorship sales, plus Stripe's payment fees.
  • Swapstack: One of the original newsletter sponsorship marketplaces, with flat-rate sponsorships, pay-per-click and affiliate deals in one place. Worth a free signup to see what matches your niche. Pricing: Free for publishers (Swapstack takes 10% on invite-only campaigns from its managed service). Paid publisher plans (Professional $30/mo, Enterprise $200/mo) are listed as coming soon.
  • Paved: A large newsletter ad network (3,000+ newsletters, per Paved). Its stated minimums of 50k subscribers or 1M monthly impressions rule out most smaller Substacks; start with Hecto or Swapstack until you're there. Pricing: Free publisher account, no subscription. Flat-fee scheduled ads need at least 50k subscribers; pay-per-click native ads need at least 1M monthly impressions.
  • Gumroad: The simplest way to sell a digital product to your readers. Fees are high per sale but there's no fixed cost. Pricing: No monthly fee; 10% + $0.50 per transaction on direct sales (profile or direct links); 30% on sales through Gumroad Discover.
  • Ko-fi: A low-friction tip jar that complements paid subscriptions. One-off tips are fee-free on Ko-fi Free, but new accounts start in Standard mode (5% on tips), so switch it off in Settings > Payment if tips are all you need. Pricing: Free to join, no monthly cost. Ko-fi Free: 0% on one-off tips and Goals, 5% on monthly tips, memberships, shop and commissions. Standard: 5% on everything, plus extras like supporter-only content and scheduled posts. PayPal/Stripe fees (usually around 3% + $0.30) apply on top. Ko-fi Gold (0% on everything for a fixed monthly fee) is still offered; price not listed on its page.

A word on browser extensions and your Substack login

Substack has no official public API for most of this, so many Substack-specific tools work through a browser extension or by holding your logged-in Substack session. That's how tools like WriteStack, StackSweller, Subshack and Byline OS's live sync function. It's common and often fine, but read each tool's privacy page, prefer read-only or CSV modes when they exist, and be wary of anything that auto-follows, auto-likes or messages people for you.
