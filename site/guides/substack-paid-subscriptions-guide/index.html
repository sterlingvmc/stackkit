Substack paid subscriptions guide: pricing, fees, taxes and paywalls (2026)

Updated 2026-11-26 · Every setting, fee and rule checked against Substack's help center and substack.com/taxes · Independent, not affiliated with Substack Inc.

The short version: connect Stripe in Settings → Payments, set a monthly and an annual price (at least $5 a month and $30 a year in US dollars), and turn on Enable payments. Substack keeps 10% and Stripe takes its processing fees, so on a $5 monthly plan you keep roughly $4. From December 1, 2026 Substack also calculates, collects and remits sales tax and VAT for you at no extra cost. Paywall part of a post to give free readers a preview, and use special offers for discounts and trials.

1. Turning on paid subscriptions

  1. Go to your publication's Settings and select Payments.
  2. Click Connect with Stripe and fill in the details Stripe asks for. You return to Substack when you're done.
  3. Set your monthly and yearly prices, and optionally a founding member price.
  4. Click the Enable payments toggle. Readers don't see that you offer paid subscriptions until you do.
  • Minimums: $5 a month and $30 a year for US-dollar Stripe accounts; other currencies may vary.
  • Both plans are required: Substack doesn't offer a yearly-only plan.
  • Change prices in Substack, not Stripe. Substack warns that editing plans in your Stripe dashboard can stop readers from subscribing and leave existing subscriptions in a bad state.

2. What you actually keep

Publishing on Substack is free. Once you charge, these fees come out of each payment (US cards, from Substack's "How much does Substack cost?" article):

FeeAmount
Substack10% of each subscription payment
Stripe card processing2.9% + $0.30 per payment
Stripe Billing (recurring payments)0.7%
Custom domain (optional)$50 one-time

Worked out on the minimum prices (our arithmetic, US card, before any tax added at checkout):

PlanSubstack 10%Stripe 2.9% + $0.30Billing 0.7%You keep
$5 monthly$0.50$0.45$0.04about $4.02 a month ($48.24 over 12 months)
$50 annual$5.00$1.75$0.35about $42.90 a year

The fixed $0.30 is charged on every payment, so it costs a monthly subscriber twelve times a year and an annual subscriber once. That's part of why it pays to make the annual plan attractive. To see the real fees on a payment, open Transactions in your Stripe dashboard and select a payment marked Succeeded; Stripe's Billing fee shows up under payouts.

The iOS app: readers can subscribe inside Substack's iOS app through Apple's in-app payments, which is on automatically for publications with payments enabled and can't be turned off while payments are connected. Apple adds a 15-30% fee. You choose Adjust prices for in-app purchases (Substack's recommendation: in-app prices go up so your payout stays close to the web price) or Keep prices the same (you absorb the fee). Substack's 10% is based on your web price either way. In the US, the app also links readers to the web checkout. Apple pays out up to 45 days after the end of each month, Apple-managed subscriptions can't be paused, and if you ever leave Substack those billing relationships don't move with you (you keep the email addresses).

3. Sales tax and VAT from December 1, 2026

Starting December 1, 2026, Substack calculates, collects and remits sales tax and VAT on paid subscriptions for you, in every country where Stripe operates. Substack's fee stays at 10% with no extra charge, and you don't need to switch anything on. Two settings are worth checking in Payments first:

  • Taxable content types: written content, video, live streams, podcasts, voiceovers, private community access and digital perks. By default the highest rate among the types you select applies to the whole price. Turn on Adjust tax according to the benefit value to split your price across types so each part is taxed at its own rate.
  • Added at checkout or included in your price: only publishers outside the US and Canada can choose inclusive pricing (tax comes out of your listed price, so you receive less). US and Canadian publishers have tax added at checkout.

US subscribers see sales tax as a separate line where it applies: new subscribers at checkout, existing ones on their receipt at renewal. Your listed price doesn't change unless you change it. Income tax on what you earn is still yours. Publishers based in 11 countries where Stripe Tax can't do this yet (including Brazil, India, Mexico and Singapore) stay responsible for their own sales tax for now; check Substack's tax page for the full list.

4. Monthly, annual and founding member plans

  • Founding member plan: an optional annual tier for readers who want to pay more. Its price can be anything higher than your annual price. Add it under Settings → Payments → Founding member → Add plan. Turn on Flexible founding plan pricing to let readers pay any amount between your annual price and the founding price.
  • What founding members can get: their own welcome email (Settings → Emails → "Welcome email to founding subscribers"), which can carry a file such as a PDF or EPUB, and founding-only posts (choose "This post is for founding members only" when you publish).
  • Removing the tier only stops new founding sign-ups; existing founding members keep renewing at their price unless Substack support lowers it at your request.

5. Changing your price later

You can change prices any time in Settings → Payments. Only new paid subscribers pay the new rate. Existing subscribers stay on the price they signed up at and renew at it unless they cancel and resubscribe; to move existing subscribers to a new price you have to ask a human at Substack support. So raising your price rewards early subscribers automatically, and a low launch price is something you live with for those readers.

6. Paywalls and free previews

  1. In the post editor, click More → Paywall and drag the line to where the free part should end.
  2. On the publish page, set the audience to Paid subscribers only (or Founding members only).
  3. To email the preview to free subscribers, keep Send via email and Substack app checked and tick Free subscribers under "Send a free preview".

Free readers get an Upgrade button and your list of subscription benefits in the email; on the web they see "This post is for paid subscribers" and a Subscribe button. You can customize the free-preview email header under "Edit email header and footer". Switching the audience back to Everyone removes the paywall.

A freewall (More → Freewall) is the free-subscriber version: readers subscribe for free to read the rest. A post can have a paywall or a freewall, not both, and freewalls work on text posts only. If you're weighing which posts to lock, our Substack SEO guide explains why evergreen posts you want found on Google are better left free.

7. Discounts, free trials and Substack Boost

  • Special offers live in Settings → Payments → Manage special offers → New offer. Each one makes a link you can share or send as a post.
  • Discounts apply to monthly and annual plans only. You set the amount and duration, and can add an end date, limit it to educational email addresses, or Automatically apply to all new subscribers (useful for a time-limited sale). Turn on Available to paid subscribers to give monthly subscribers the discount automatically when they upgrade to annual.
  • Free trials also come from a special-offer link. Readers must enter card details, and they're charged when the trial ends unless they cancel.
  • Track redemptions at your-publication.substack.com/publish/offers.
  • Boost appears in Settings → Growth once payments are on. With it, Substack shows offers for you: an upsell email to a small, highly engaged slice of free subscribers (about 0.1% of your list a day, 20% off for a year by default), a discount of up to 20% shown to readers who just paid for another Boost publication, and a free month offered to paid subscribers who are about to cancel. You can switch off the individual options or Boost entirely.

Every discount is also given to readers who would have paid full price, and locked-in prices last as long as the subscription does. Decide what a sale is for (new subscribers, monthly-to-annual upgrades, win-backs) before you pick the percentage.

8. Tracking revenue and selling extras

Substack's Stats page has an Earnings tab with your Stripe payout history, and the iOS app shows total revenue under Creator stats. If you want churn, MRR and cohort numbers beyond that, the tools below read your Substack data or exports. For one-off income next to a paid tier, sell a guide on Gumroad or take tips on Ko-fi, and see our sponsorships guide for sponsor income.

9. Checklist before you turn on payments

  1. Write down what paid subscribers get, one sentence per benefit, so you can say it plainly in your launch post and paywall previews.
  2. Pick monthly and annual prices (at least $5 and $30) and decide whether to add a founding tier.
  3. Choose your in-app pricing option.
  4. Check your taxable content types in Payments before December 1.
  5. Write the welcome emails for paid and founding subscribers.
  6. Draft a launch post with a paywalled section so free readers see what they'd unlock.

Tools mentioned in this guide

  • Byline OS: Paid-newsletter writers who want MRR, churn, cohort and revenue-per-post analytics. Starter free (CSV import, 30-day retention); Growth $19/mo; Scale $49/mo. 14-day trial. Currently in private beta.
  • StackStats: Writers who want churn risk, cohort retention, superfan and growth-source analysis from their own Substack exports. 1-year license $39 (renew updates for $29/yr; the app keeps working if you don't); Lifetime $79 early-adopter price (listed down from $129). One-time payments, no subscription; 14-day refund.
  • Substack Control Center: Writers who want a cheap, one-tab dashboard for net adds, churn, MRR and which posts or Notes drive signups, without a monthly subscription. $25 one-time (Chrome extension; lifetime updates; 30-day money-back). Also available in a $89 Growth Bundle with Substack Pro Studio.
  • Gumroad: Selling ebooks, templates and guides alongside your newsletter. No monthly fee; 10% + $0.50 per transaction on direct sales (profile or direct links); 30% on sales through Gumroad Discover.
  • Ko-fi: One-off tips from readers who won't commit to a paid subscription. Free to join, no monthly cost. Ko-fi Free: 0% on one-off tips and Goals, 5% on monthly tips, memberships, shop and commissions. Standard: 5% on everything, plus extras like supporter-only content and scheduled posts. PayPal/Stripe fees (usually around 3% + $0.30) apply on top. Ko-fi Gold (0% on everything for a fixed monthly fee) is still offered; price not listed on its page.
