Substack Notes schedulers compared (2026)

Updated 2026-10-30 · 10 options, including Substack's own scheduler · Prices checked on each vendor's site · Independent, no affiliate links on this page

The short answer: if you post a few Notes a week, use Substack's built-in scheduler. It's free, and Notes go out from Substack's own servers. Pay for a tool only when you want to batch a week or more at once: Buffer if Notes are one channel among several, SubflowAI if you want the cheapest batch queue for Substack alone, and WriteStack if you want the most complete Substack-only Notes suite.

Start with the free built-in scheduler

Substack schedules Notes natively on the web, iOS and Android. On the web, write your Note, click the calendar icon, choose Schedule and pick a time in your local time zone (on the apps, tap "…" first). Scheduled Notes appear in your Drafts tab, where you can edit them until they publish. The catch: you schedule one Note at a time, with no queue or calendar view. If that's fine for you, you don't need anything on this page.

How to choose

  • You post to X, LinkedIn or Threads too: a multi-platform scheduler saves the most time. Buffer's free plan covers up to 3 channels, and Typefully suits writers who start on X. Both free plans include Notes; see Buffer vs Typefully for Substack Notes.
  • Substack is your whole world and you want to batch cheaply: SubflowAI (about $1.17/mo on its 5-year plan) or NotesIQ ($4.99 one time, but very new).
  • You want analytics and scheduling in one place: NoteStack or WriteStack. Both rank Notes by the subscribers they bring, not just likes.
  • You start from long-form posts and publish beyond Substack: Narrareach turns articles into Notes and cross-posts to other platforms.
  • You only want to know which Notes work: skip scheduling and use a read-only tool such as Subshack (free) or Substack Insights. See our Substack Insights vs NoteStack comparison.

Know how a tool publishes before you install it

Most Substack-only schedulers work through your logged-in Substack session: a browser extension acting as you, or a service holding your session. That's how this category works, not a red flag by itself, but read each tool's privacy page, prefer tools that never post without your approval, and avoid anything that follows, likes or messages people automatically. If a tool stops working after a Substack change, the built-in scheduler is always there as a fallback.

A note on AI-written Notes

Several of these tools can draft Notes from your articles "in your voice". Readers follow you for how you think, so treat AI drafts as a starting point and rewrite them before scheduling. Batch scheduling helps you stay consistent; it doesn't replace replying to people, which is where much of the growth on Notes comes from.

Our verdicts

  • Buffer: Reliable and affordable, and its free plan includes Substack Notes scheduling. The best default if you post to several networks.
  • Typefully: A polished writing-first scheduler that now publishes and schedules Substack Notes on every plan, including free. It handles Notes only (no long-form posts, no video in Notes), and its paid analytics are for X, so it suits writers who start on X or LinkedIn more than Notes-first writers.
  • Narrareach: Good if distribution beyond Substack is your bottleneck. Hands Notes to Substack's native scheduler rather than posting from a stored session.
  • SubflowAI: The cheapest serious batch Notes scheduler we've found (the 5-year plan works out to about $1.17/mo), and Notes are queued on Substack's own scheduler so they publish with your laptop closed. It's a one-person product that works through your logged-in Substack session (the Claude/ChatGPT Connect feature uses Substack's unofficial API), and the AI-in-your-voice features are optional: edit anything it drafts before it goes out.
  • NotesIQ: The cheapest Notes analytics extension we've found, and it keeps data in your browser with no account. Very new (v1.0.3, launched Sept 2026, no Chrome Web Store ratings yet) from a solo developer, and its price has already changed since launch, so treat it as an inexpensive experiment. Scheduling publishes through Substack's own composer in your logged-in session.
  • NoteStack: A lot of Notes analytics for the price, with data kept in your browser. Be aware the scheduler auto-publishes through your logged-in Substack session, and the AI features send your Notes text to its server and Anthropic. Skip the Ghostwriter if your voice is the product.
  • WriteStack: The most complete Substack-only Notes suite: batch scheduling, voice-matched Note drafts, analytics on which Notes bring subscribers, a filtered notification inbox, and now an in-app writing coach (Quill) plus an MCP server so Claude or ChatGPT can draft and queue Notes. Pricey for a beginner, and publishing still runs through its browser extension acting in your logged-in session (you click Sync to send scheduled Notes).
  • StackBuddy: A Notes scheduler with a free plan that keeps scheduling, and its Chrome extension hands each Note to Substack's own scheduler, so Notes publish from Substack's servers with your computer off. Pro adds voice-matching AI drafts, analytics and LinkedIn cross-posting. Small one-developer tool; the extension acts in your logged-in Substack session and syncs your existing Notes to StackBuddy's dashboard, so read its privacy page first.
  • StackSweller: Does one job (a Notes queue that runs with your laptop closed). Be aware it works by holding your Substack session, and Substack now schedules single Notes natively.
